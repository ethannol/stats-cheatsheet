==============================================================================
R^2=0.928  AIC=-118.8  n=68

Implied warming per CO2 doubling (slope * ln 2): 2.71 C (OLS 95% CI 2.53 to 2.90)
  > The slope is the warming per e-fold of CO2; times ln(2) it is warming per doubling:
    2.7 C here. The IPCC transient climate response is ~1.8 C (likely 1.4-2.2), so this
    single-predictor fit overshoots. Why: methane, N2O, halocarbons and declining aerosol
    cooling all rose alongside CO2, and the coefficient absorbs every one of them (omitted
    variable bias). The regression is a useful descriptive fit; it is not an attribution
    study. Physics plus a model of all forcings is what makes the IPCC number defensible.

Standard errors under autocorrelation: OLS vs HAC vs GLS-AR(1)
==============================================================================
                           slope      SE        t
OLS                       3.9134  0.1339  29.2284

HAC (Newey-West, 5 lags)  3.9134  0.1921  20.3699
GLSAR(1) Cochrane-Orcutt  3.9921  0.1776  22.4814
GLSAR estimated rho = 0.28
  > Autocorrelation does not bias the slope but the naive OLS SE is too small by roughly
    sqrt(n / n_eff). HAC (Newey-West) and GLS with AR(1) errors both widen the interval.
    The conclusion survives here because the signal is enormous; for marginal trends it
    often does not. Interviewers like to hear 'coefficients unbiased, SEs wrong' in one
    breath.

In T~lnco2+year: coef(lnco2)=8.36 (was 3.91 alone), p(lnco2)=0.000, p(year)=0.000, corr(lnco2, year)=0.995, VIF=91
  > ln(CO2) beats a linear time trend (lower AIC/BIC) because CO2 growth accelerated and
    so did warming. Adding 'year' still improves AIC, but with r=0.995 between the
    predictors the VIF is 91 and the CO2 coefficient swings from 3.9 to 8.4:
    multicollinearity makes individual coefficients unstable even when the joint fit is
    excellent. Observational data alone cannot separate 'CO2' from 'anything else that
    rose smoothly'.

  > Elevation coefficient -3.38 C/km is a surface lapse rate. It is shallower than the

OLS on prcp:      R2=0.572  Breusch-Pagan p=1.4e-04

coefficients (multiplicative scale):
           log-OLS  Gamma GLM log-link
Intercept   9.3122              8.8123
lat        -0.0053             -0.0041
elev_km    -0.5044             -0.5678
lon         0.0243              0.0184
  > Annual precipitation is strictly positive and heteroscedastic (wet places vary more).
    Log-transforming y or using a Gamma GLM with a log link gives multiplicative effects
    (coefficient = fractional change per unit of x). The two sets of coefficients agree
    closely. Breusch-Pagan still rejects after the log because the variance also depends
    on longitude (a wet East / dry West split that lat+lon+elev cannot fully describe);
    the GLM handles mean-variance coupling by construction and predicts on the natural
    scale without the back-transform bias of exp(E[log y]).
