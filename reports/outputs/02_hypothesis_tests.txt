==============================================================================
Pitfall: testing on daily values instead of seasonal means
==============================================================================
Daily values: n=2760 vs 2760, t=8.6, p=8.1e-18
Seasonal means: n=30 vs 30, t=3.7, p=5.3e-04
lag-1 autocorrelation of JJA daily TMIN: 0.75
  > Same data, same mean difference, but the daily-level p-value is ~14 orders of
    magnitude smaller because it pretends to have 2,760 independent observations per
    period. Days within a summer are autocorrelated (and share the summer's anomaly), so
    the effective sample size is far lower. Aggregate to the unit that is plausibly
    independent, or model the dependence explicitly (mixed model, GLS).
Bootstrap 95% CI for difference in means: (0.34, 1.12)  vs Welch CI (0.33, 1.14)

odds ratio (late vs early) = 1.49
Warm nights per summer: early median=28 late median=36  Mann-Whitney p=0.0031  (n=30 vs 30 summers)
  > Chi-square and the two-proportion z-test are the same test for a 2x2 table (z^2 =
    chi2). Both inherit the daily-independence problem. The cleaner design is one count
    per summer compared with Mann-Whitney or a Poisson / negative-binomial GLM with a
    period term.

  > Running 12 tests at alpha=0.05 gives a 46% chance of at least one false positive under
    H0. Bonferroni divides alpha by m (controls the family-wise error rate; conservative
    when tests are correlated). Benjamini-Hochberg controls the expected fraction of false
    discoveries among rejections; it is the default for screening many hypotheses. Months
    that survive BH but not Bonferroni are the typical grey zone.

Power analysis: how many summers to detect a given TMIN shift?
==============================================================================
  shift 0.25 C (d=0.32): n=153 per group for 80% power
  shift 0.50 C (d=0.64): n=39 per group for 80% power
  shift 1.00 C (d=1.29): n=11 per group for 80% power
  achieved power for observed d=0.95 with n=30: 0.950
    [fig] figures/02_hypothesis_tests/power_curves.png
  > Power scales with (effect size / SD) and sqrt(n). A 0.25 C shift needs >100 summers
    per group at this station's interannual variability: the signal-to-noise problem is
    why climate detection relies on spatial averaging and long records rather than single
    sites. Post-hoc 'observed power' is just a transform of the p-value; plan power in
    advance.
