  > Base metals run at 21-28% annualised volatility, gold at 12%. Returns are fat-tailed,
    so a normal model understates the frequency of crashes; copper and zinc move together
    (macro demand) while gold is nearly uncorrelated with the industrial metals, which is
    why a diversified miner's cash-flow volatility is lower than any single commodity's.

Random walk or mean reversion? Copper and gold
==============================================================================
copper   ADF p (unit root)=0.630  AR(1) b=0.9965  half-life=16.6 yr  long-run mean=10,054
gold     ADF p (unit root)=0.991  AR(1) b=1.0022 >= 1: no mean reversion estimable (pure random walk or trending)
  > Neither series rejects a unit root at conventional levels over 35 years of monthly
    data: the power to distinguish slow mean reversion (half-life of several years) from a
    random walk is low. Yet the two assumptions give very different 10-year price fans and
    NPV distributions, so state the assumption, show both, and justify reversion with the
    cost curve (incentive price) rather than with a statistical test that cannot settle
    it.
    [fig] figures/19_economics/price_fans.png
10-year 90% band width: GBM 9.2x, mean reversion 6.2x

SYNTHETIC copper project: deterministic vs Monte Carlo NPV
==============================================================================
base case NPV = $0.91 billion at copper 8,916 $/t

tornado (+-10% one at a time, $bn):
                  low  high  swing
input                             
discount         1.03  0.79   0.25
capex            1.07  0.75   0.32
tonnes_per_year  0.65  1.16   0.50
opex_per_t       1.19  0.62   0.57
grade_pct        0.37  1.44   1.07
recovery         0.37  1.44   1.07
price            0.37  1.44   1.07

Monte Carlo (20,000 runs): mean NPV=$0.91bn, median=$0.73bn, P10=$-0.72bn, P90=$2.75bn, P(NPV<0)=26.9%
  > The tornado says price and grade dominate; costs and capex matter less because they
    are spread over the life or discounted. The Monte Carlo adds what the tornado cannot:
    joint uncertainty and correlation (high prices come with high costs, so the upside is
    clipped), and asymmetric inputs (capex overruns). The result is a mean NPV of $0.91bn
    against a deterministic $0.91bn and a 27% chance of destroying value, which is the
    number the board needs.
    [fig] figures/19_economics/npv.png

==============================================================================
Break-even cut-off grade
==============================================================================
g_c = opex / (price x recovery x payability) = 0.292% Cu at 8,916 $/t; at -30% price: 0.417%
  > The cut-off grade converts the grade-tonnage curve (section 15) into ore tonnes and
    metal, and it moves with price: a price fall raises the cut-off, shrinks the ore
    inventory and lowers the mine life, which is why resource statements quote the price
    assumption.

==============================================================================
Real-option flavour: value of waiting two years for price information
==============================================================================
commit now: expected NPV $0.91bn (P(<0)=32%); wait 2 years and build only if positive: $0.98bn -> option value $+0.06bn
  > Waiting trades two years of discounting against the right not to build into a price
    collapse. When volatility is high and the project is marginal the option is worth a
    lot; for a deeply profitable project it is worth little and delay is pure cost. This
    is the intuition behind why miners sequence projects and hold undeveloped resources.
