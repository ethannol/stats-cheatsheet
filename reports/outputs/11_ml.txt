  > Read the table top-down. Climatology explains the seasonal cycle; persistence is
    nearly unbeatable one day ahead because rivers are integrators. Weather-only models
    answer a different and more interesting question: how much of the flow is explained by
    the last few months of weather at one gauge? All five land at R2 0.66-0.67 in log
    space: when a linear model ties gradient boosting, the ceiling is the information in
    the features (one rain gauge for an 11,500 sq mi basin), not model flexibility. Adding
    persistence to the boosted model gives the best one-day forecast. NSE rewards matching
    big floods; KGE also penalises bias and variability errors.

==============================================================================
shuffled KFold R2:    0.792 +- 0.007
TimeSeriesSplit R2:   0.612 +- 0.087  (60-day gap between train and test)
true holdout 2011-25: 0.664
  > Shuffled K-fold puts Tuesday in the training set and Wednesday in the test set; with
    30-120 day rolling features they are almost the same row, so the score is inflated.
    The time-aware split with a gap matches the real holdout. This is the single most
    common evaluation mistake with environmental data.

  > The top features are sin_doy, api, tmax30, p120: the seasonal cycle of
    evapotranspiration, the exponentially weighted antecedent precipitation index, recent
    temperature (again evapotranspiration) and the 120-day rainfall total. Short windows
    (1-7 days) matter little because a single DC gauge is a poor proxy for a storm over an
    11,500 sq mi basin, while months of accumulated rainfall control baseflow. The partial
    dependence plots show the shape of each effect (flow rises with p120, falls with
    tmax30). Permutation importance on held-out data avoids the impurity-importance bias
    toward continuous, high-cardinality features; correlated features (p60/p120/api) share
    importance, so read groups, not individual bars.
