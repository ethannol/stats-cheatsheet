Autocorrelation and Mann-Kendall: size (no trend) and power (trend) of four variants
==============================================================================
           raw MK  simple PW  TFPW (Yue)  Hamed-Rao
rho test                                           
0.0 size    0.048      0.051       0.050      0.059
    power   0.825      0.732       0.824      0.788
0.3 size    0.124      0.053       0.150      0.090
    power   0.738      0.378       0.740      0.578
0.6 size    0.327      0.043       0.415      0.094
    power   0.585      0.117       0.652      0.273
  > Size rows should read 0.05. Raw MK rejects a true null ~30% of the time at rho=0.6.
    Simple pre-whitening fixes the size but, as the power rows show, throws away part of a
    real trend. Yue's trend-free pre-whitening (TFPW) was designed to keep the power, but
    under the null it adds a spurious Sen slope back onto whitened noise and over-rejects
    even more than raw MK (Bayazit & Onoz 2007; Hamed 2009). The Hamed-Rao variance
    correction holds the size near nominal with a smaller power cost. Knowing which
    'standard fix' is actually broken is the kind of thing that separates practitioners
    from tutorial-readers.

GISTEMP hinge: breakpoint=1975, slope before=+0.037/decade, after=+0.206/decade
AIC linear=-61.3  AIC hinge=-204.1  (hinge uses 2 extra params incl. breakpoint)
  > Pettitt finds the year that best splits the series into two medians; on a series with
    a gradual trend it will always 'find' a change near the middle, so it cannot by itself
    distinguish shift from trend. The hinge (broken-stick) model estimates where the slope
    changed; for global temperature the acceleration lands in the 1970s. The breakpoint is
    itself estimated, so count it as a parameter when comparing AIC.

  > Starting in the 1998 El Nino year was the basis of the 'warming hiatus' claim (which
    has since filled in: the 1998-2025 trend is now the steepest in the table). For the
    11-year window the HAC interval runs 0.11 to 0.49 C/decade, from well below to nearly
    triple the 1950-2025 rate. Short-window trends are endpoint noise; the honest answer
    is the CI, and the honest design fixes the window before looking.
