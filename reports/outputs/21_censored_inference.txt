  > The first era reported 69% of lead results as below 1 or 5 ug/L; the latest era
    detects almost everything, with a median of 0.14 ug/L. Substituting the detection
    limit turns the early era into a column of 1s and 5s and makes the decrease look
    overwhelming (p ~ 1e-15); substituting zero turns the same results into a column of
    zeros and reverses the sign. Gehan's test only uses pairs whose order is certain: an
    early '< 1' against a recent 0.3 is not evidence either way, because the early value
    could have been 0.1, while an early detected 3 against a recent 0.3 is. What survives
    is a decrease with p = 0.0018: real, but with far less evidence than substitution
    claims. When censoring fractions differ between groups, as here, the Peto-Prentice
    score test weights the pairs more evenly than Gehan; both are in Helsel (2012) chapter
    9 and the NADA package.

  > The UCL95 is the exposure-point concentration in a risk assessment: a one-sided limit
    that the true mean exceeds with 5% probability if the sampling is representative. The
    spread between the Student-t (22.60) and the Chebyshev 99% (34.57) is the cost of not
    knowing the distribution. ProUCL's decision table picks by fit and skew because the
    t-interval under-covers for skewed data and Land's lognormal H-UCL can exceed the
    maximum observation. With non-detects the mean and its standard error come from
    Kaplan-Meier and the same choice between t and Chebyshev follows. One caveat the
    software does not print: a UCL assumes independent samples, and 75 river samples over
    eight years are seasonal and serially correlated, so the effective n is smaller than
    it looks.

Prediction and tolerance limits (Unified Guidance mechanics): is a new sample unusual?
==============================================================================
background = 2006-15 total arsenic, n=83 (log scale; Shapiro p on logs = 0.032)
  upper prediction limit, 1 future sample:    39.93 ug/L   (nonparametric: background max 73.20, confidence 99%)
  upper prediction limit, 75 future samples (Bonferroni): 85.39 ug/L
  upper tolerance limit, 95% coverage / 95% confidence: 45.45 ug/L
  2016-23 samples (n=75): 4 exceed the one-sample limit, 1 exceed the 75-sample limit, 4 exceed the tolerance limit
  > A prediction limit bounds the next sample(s); a tolerance limit bounds a stated
    fraction of the population. Compliance monitoring under the Unified Guidance tests
    each new downgradient sample against a prediction limit built from independent
    background, with the number of future comparisons folded into the limit (Bonferroni
    here; the guidance uses exact kappa tables) and usually a retest rule. Testing 75 new
    samples one at a time against a one-sample limit would expect about 3.8 false
    exceedances; the k-sample limit is wider for that reason. River samples are not
    independent background, so 4 exceedances of the one-sample limit are a demonstration
    of the mechanics, not a finding.
