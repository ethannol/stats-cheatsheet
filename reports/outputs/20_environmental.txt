==============================================================================
Concentration-discharge relationship
==============================================================================
log10 C = -0.59 + 0.90 log10 Q   (R2=0.53, n=309)
a doubling of flow multiplies copper by 1.87
monthly median Cu (ug/L): 1:38, 2:62, 3:36, 4:38, 5:76, 6:79, 7:19, 8:14, 9:38, 10:16, 11:16, 12:24
  > The C-Q slope is +0.90: concentration *rises* with flow. That is mobilisation, not
    dilution: high water scours copper-laden tailings and streambank sediments deposited
    by a century of mining, the defining signature of this Superfund river. A point-source
    discharge would show the opposite sign. Any trend analysis must first remove this flow
    dependence.

  > Exceedance fractions with Wilson intervals are the honest compliance summary; 'number
    of exceedances this year' is a count without a denominator. The decline from 97% to
    85% is real but the river still exceeds this benchmark in most samples at any flow: a
    chronic sediment-sourced problem, not an event-driven one, so the C-Q slope tells you
    when it is worst, not when it is compliant. The criterion (13.0 ug/L) is the
    hardness-100 chronic value for illustration; the real standard moves with each
    sample's hardness, so compliance assessment needs paired hardness measurements.

  > The CUSUM turns in 1996: flow-adjusted copper ran above its long-term level before and
    below it after. Upstream, the Warm Springs Ponds treatment system (which settles
    metals out of Silver Bow Creek before it becomes the Clark Fork) was rebuilt and
    expanded in the early- to-mid 1990s; the 2010s floodplain tailings removals are a
    second candidate for the later decline. A change point found after the fact is a
    hypothesis to test against the intervention record, not a proof. For cadmium the non-
    detect fraction was 60-70% in the early eras with changing detection limits, so only
    censored estimators (Kaplan-Meier) give comparable statistics across eras; the naive
    'DL as value' means are 2-3x too high.
