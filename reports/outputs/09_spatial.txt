  > Raw temperature is massively autocorrelated (of course: latitude). The interesting
    test is on the residuals of the lat+elev+lon model: I is still clearly positive,
    meaning the model's errors cluster (coastal strips, basins, the Gulf). The
    regression's standard errors in section 03 were therefore too small, exactly as with
    time-series autocorrelation.

Empirical variogram of the residuals and model fits
==============================================================================
spherical:   nugget=0.522 sill=1.746 range=2889 km
exponential: nugget=0.470 sill=1.750 range=4140 km (practical)
residual variance = 1.576
    [fig] figures/09_spatial/variogram.png
  > The nugget (0.52 C^2) is variance at zero distance: station siting, instrument
    differences, microclimate. Semivariance rises toward the sill over ~2889 km, the
    distance beyond which residuals are uncorrelated. The empirical curve keeps climbing
    past the sample variance at large lags: a sign that the lat+elev+lon trend has not
    removed all the large-scale structure (non-stationarity). Fit variograms only to
    roughly half the domain, and treat an unbounded variogram as a prompt to improve the
    trend model.

==============================================================================
Interpolation: IDW vs regression + kriged residuals
==============================================================================
    [fig] figures/09_spatial/interpolation_maps.png
  > IDW produces bulls-eyes around stations and cannot extrapolate a trend (mountain cold
    spots vanish between stations). Regression kriging uses the physics (lapse rate,
    latitude) for the large-scale pattern and kriging for the spatially correlated
    leftovers, and it hands you a map of its own uncertainty. The elevation grid here is
    IDW of station elevations, which is crude; use a DEM in real work.

Leave-one-out cross-validation RMSE (C)
==============================================================================
                     RMSE    MAE
regression kriging  0.803  0.537
OK on tavg          1.256  0.769
OLS trend only      1.274  0.911
IDW p=2             2.045  1.478
IDW p=1             3.720  2.952
  > Each leave-one-out fold refits the trend and both variograms on training stations
    only. Leave-one-out measures local interpolation; spatial block holdouts are needed to
    assess transfer across regions. It is a useful yardstick for interpolators (fit at
    data points is trivially perfect for IDW and kriging). Combining a covariate trend
    with kriged residuals beats either ingredient alone; ordinary kriging on raw
    temperature struggles because the field is not stationary (the mean trends with
    latitude), which the variogram assumes away.
